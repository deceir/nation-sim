# Diplomatia economic balance targets

The economy is tuned around hourly turns and these intended player horizons:

- **First session:** understand quotas, make a Province investment, and see every turn produce useful output.
- **First 1–3 days:** afford early Infrastructure, Province upgrades, or an accessible Project.
- **First week:** establish the second Province; its commodity requirement is covered by the starter runway while its cash cost still requires activity.
- **Following weeks:** specialize Provinces, trade around geographic deposit strengths, and prepare the escalating third and later Provinces.
- **Long term:** complete major National Projects after accumulating or trading for several hundred relevant commodities.

## Current balance surfaces

- New nations receive 500 units of common primary commodities, 75 Construction Materials, 100 Processed Foods, and 75 Basic Goods.
- Default quotas demonstrate production immediately: 35% Processed Foods, 45% Construction Materials, and 20% Basic Goods.
- Normal starting geography produces enough Foodstuffs for population upkeep. Antarctica is intentionally harsh but has a large starting buffer.
- Yen values use a 100× nominal scale so revenues and costs feel appropriately national without changing purchasing power or progression time.
- Province founding costs use `¥22,500,000 × N^2.6` and `40 Construction Materials × N^2.2`, before Gear and policy modifiers.
- Resource quantities are measured in metric tonnes; the redenomination does not alter resource production, consumption, or requirements.
- Geographic richness ranges from 0.45 to 1.50. Every primary resource remains producible, but optimal chains differ materially by continent.
- National Project commodity requirements are 12% of the original first-pass figures. Cash, construction time, slots, and specialization opportunity cost remain the dominant long-term gates.

Player-set market prices mean resource bonuses cannot have a fixed Yen payback. Balance should therefore be monitored using median market prices, days-to-afford, quota utilization, food-shortage frequency, and the percentage of Project inputs acquired through trade.
